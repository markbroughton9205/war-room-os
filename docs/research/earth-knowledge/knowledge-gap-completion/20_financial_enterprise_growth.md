## Executive Summary

This domain is no longer missing. It can be built around real mechanism-level infrastructure: household/SME finance (OECD, World Bank Findex, IMF), business environment/development finance (World Bank B-READY, IFC, AfDB, ADB, IDB Invest, EBRD), macro/capital data (BIS), accounting/consolidation (IFRS), legal-entity ownership (GLEIF), exchanges/securities (WFE/IOSCO), tax administration (OECD), a machine-readable national corporate-registry exemplar (Companies House), merger regulation (European Commission), and university corporate-finance/valuation data/training (NYU Stern/MIT) [FN01–FN21].

This supports Stages 0–10 without promising wealth. Outcomes remain constrained by jurisdiction, tax, financing terms, competition law, dilution, liquidity, execution and loss risk.

## Scope

Stabilization; income; small business; capital formation; asset ownership; scaling; acquisitions; corporate finance; capital markets; holding companies; and large-scale ownership/capital allocation. Non-U.S. systems are explicit through global institutions and regional development banks.

## Stage Architecture

### Stage 0 — Stabilization
- **Mechanisms:** cash-flow visibility, budget/records, emergency liquidity, debt inventory, credit, insurance, tax compliance and banking access.
- **Source spine:** FN01 OECD financial education; FN02 World Bank Findex; FN03 IMF FAS; local central-bank/consumer-regulator/tax adapters.
- **Exit evidence:** reconciled monthly cash flow; emergency-reserve rule; debt/interest map; insurance inventory; tax-record folder; no unknown recurring obligations.

### Stage 1 — Income
- **Mechanisms:** employment, skill premiums, contracting/freelancing, sales, trades, digital work and entrepreneurship convert labor/skill into cash flow.
- **Source spine:** B-READY [FN04], national labor/licensing/tax systems, regional SME/development agencies [FN18–FN21].
- **Exit evidence:** documented income channels; after-tax unit economics; skill plan; legal/licensing requirements.

### Stage 2 — Small Business
- **Mechanisms:** legal formation, model, bookkeeping/accounting, pricing, gross/contribution margin, sales, marketing, operations, hiring and working capital.
- **Source spine:** B-READY [FN04], IFC MSME [FN06], IFRS/SME reporting pathways [FN08], local registry/tax authority; Companies House [FN14] is a machine-readable registry exemplar.
- **Exit evidence:** separate books/bank records; monthly statements; priced offer with viable unit economics; tax/filing calendar; repeatable operating process.

### Stage 3 — Capital Formation
- **Mechanisms:** retained earnings; business credit/debt; grants/development finance; angels/VC/PE; equity or crowdfunding where lawful. Debt preserves ownership but adds fixed obligations; equity shares risk but dilutes ownership/control.
- **Source spine:** OECD SME finance [FN05], IFC [FN06], AfDB/ADB/IDB Invest/EBRD [FN18–FN21], FAS/Findex [FN02–FN03].
- **Exit evidence:** use-of-funds plan; repayment/dilution model; downside scenario; eligibility/terms map.

### Stage 4 — Asset Ownership
- **Mechanisms:** productive/financial assets can create cash flow, appreciation or strategic control; risk/liquidity differ by asset.
- **Source spine:** BIS [FN07], WFE [FN11], IOSCO/member regulators [FN12], GLEIF [FN10], local land/IP/securities authorities.
- **Exit evidence:** asset register with owner/entity, cost basis, cash flow, debt, insurance, liquidity, tax treatment and concentration risk.

### Stage 5 — Scaling
- **Mechanisms:** management systems, delegation, procurement, logistics/supply chain, automation, governance, risk controls and repeatable economics increase output without proportional founder labor.
- **Source spine:** B-READY [FN04], IFC/MDBs [FN06/FN18–FN21], IFRS [FN08].
- **Exit evidence:** KPI tree; delegated roles; SOPs; working-capital forecast; concentration analysis; governance cadence.

### Stage 6 — Acquisitions
- **Mechanisms:** valuation, due diligence, purchase structure, seller financing/debt/equity, regulatory clearance, integration and operating improvement. Leverage can amplify gains **and** insolvency/downside risk.
- **Source spine:** IFRS 3/10 [FN09], EU merger control [FN15], Damodaran [FN16], current national competition/company/tax authorities.
- **Exit evidence:** valuation range; quality-of-earnings review; debt capacity; legal/regulatory checklist; integration plan; downside/failed-synergy scenario.

### Stage 7 — Corporate Finance
- **Mechanisms:** financial statements, FCF, ROIC, cost of capital, capital structure, treasury, M&A and governance connect operations to enterprise value.
- **Source spine:** IFRS [FN08–FN09], BIS [FN07], Damodaran/MIT [FN16–FN17].
- **Exit evidence:** integrated statements; cash-flow bridge; capital-allocation policy; ROIC/cost-of-capital comparison; liquidity/covenant monitoring.

### Stage 8 — Capital Markets
- **Mechanisms:** IPO/equity issuance, debt markets, listing, public reporting and institutional capital trade private flexibility for access to deeper capital and public obligations.
- **Source spine:** WFE [FN11], IOSCO/member regulators [FN12], IFRS [FN08], BIS [FN07].
- **Exit evidence:** jurisdiction listing/reporting/regulator map; alternatives; dilution/debt service analysis; governance readiness.

### Stage 9 — Holding Companies
- **Mechanisms:** parent/subsidiary structures separate operating companies and can centralize treasury/services/governance while preserving entity-level liabilities/accounting. International structures add tax, substance, transfer-pricing and regulatory complexity; there is no universally "best" holdco jurisdiction.
- **Source spine:** IFRS 10/control [FN09], GLEIF ownership [FN10], national registries/tax authorities, OECD tax context [FN13].
- **Exit evidence:** entity map; ownership percentages; intercompany agreements; consolidated reporting logic; treasury controls; local legal/tax review.

### Stage 10 — Large-Scale Ownership
- **Real mechanisms:** founder equity, retained ownership through growth, reinvestment of free cash flow, acquisitions, network/economies-of-scale effects, defensible IP/infrastructure, prudent leverage, capital allocation and long-duration compounding.
- **Key distinction:** **enterprise value is not spendable cash.** Founder wealth can be illiquid and can fall with operating performance, dilution, debt, market conditions or loss of control. Liquidity usually requires distributions/dividends, sale, recapitalization or public-market transactions subject to law/tax/contracts.
- **Exit evidence:** portfolio capital-allocation model; liquidity map; concentration/downside analysis; succession/governance plan; independent legal/tax/accounting review when needed.

## Real Wealth-Building vs Speculation vs Hype

| Class | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| Real mechanism | Legal ownership is clear; source of return is identifiable (cash flow, productivity, contractual yield, enterprise growth); risks/liquidity/tax/dilution modeled; records auditable. |
| Speculative | Return depends heavily on future resale price, uncertain adoption, leverage, binary outcomes or unproven economics. It must be labeled and risk assumptions explicit. |
| Get-rich-quick hype | Guaranteed/high-return claims with little risk/work; valuation treated as cash; dilution/tax/liquidity ignored; urgency/secrecy; exceptional outcome presented as normal. Flag, do not normalize. |## Geographic Coverage
